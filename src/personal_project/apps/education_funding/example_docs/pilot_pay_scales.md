# UK Airline Pilot Pay Scales: Cadet, First Officer, and Captain

> Research compiled April 2026. Figures are gross annual base salary unless stated otherwise.
> "Total comp" includes sector pay, duty pay, and allowances where noted.

---

## 1. Cadet and Trainee Pay During Training

No major UK airline pays a salary during ab-initio flight training. Airlines that run fully-funded schemes cover tuition and provide accommodation or subsistence support, but cadets receive no wage until they qualify and begin flying the line.

| Airline | Scheme Type | Pay During Training | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **British Airways** | Fully-funded (Speedbird Pilot Academy) | None; accommodation + subsistence provided | BA pays all tuition (~£100K+). On qualification, cadets start at a reduced salary of ~£37,000–£40,000 (vs ~£73,000–£78,000 for external hires) — the reduced rate implicitly recovers the training investment. Up to 200 places per year as of 2025. |
| **TUI Airways** | Fully-funded MPL | None — no salary or allowances during ~19-month training | Post-qualification SO headline salary is £68,459 (pre-deduction). Training costs are deducted pre-tax from salary over 4 years. Cadets who leave within 4 years must repay. |
| **easyJet** | Self-funded MPL | None; cadets self-fund ~£100,000+ training | On qualifying as Second Officer: £57,235–£60,440/year. No sector pay at SO grade. Loan repayments of ~£1,000/month apply for most cadets. |
| **Jet2** | Sponsored cadet scheme | Not publicly disclosed; presumed nil | Post-qualification FO from ~£62,621. Places highly competitive. |
| **Wizz Air** | Wizz Air Pilot Academy (WAPA) | None | Post-qualification FO earns ~€60,000 total initially. Cadets repay €47,510 tuition at stepped rates: €520/month (months 1–30), €897/month (months 31–60), €1,000/month (months 61–65). |
| **Ryanair** | None | N/A | Recruits only type-rated pilots; no ab-initio scheme. |
| **Virgin Atlantic** | Future Flyers — suspended | N/A | Programme under redevelopment; no active intake as of April 2026. |
| **Loganair** | None | N/A | Recruits experienced pilots only. |

## 2. Post-Qualification Pay Progression by Airline

### 2.1 British Airways (Mainline)

BA operates a seniority-based pay scale. Short-haul (A320 family) and long-haul (B787, A350, B777, A380) commands carry different rates.

| Career Stage | Approx. Timeline | Base Salary (£/year) | Total Comp Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| New FO — Cadet entry route (short-haul) | Year 1–2 post-qual | ~£37,000–£40,000 (reduced rate) | ~£45,000–£55,000 |
| Junior FO — External/direct entry | Year 1–3 | £73,102–£78,000 | £85,000–£95,000 |
| Experienced FO (short-haul) | Year 3–7 | £78,000–£89,520 | £90,000–£105,000 |
| Senior FO / Long-haul FO | Year 7–12 | £89,520–£115,000 | £105,000–£135,000 |
| Short-haul Captain | Year 10–15 | £130,000–£145,000 | £145,000–£165,000 |
| Long-haul Captain | Year 12–20+ | £145,000–£167,000+ | £175,000–£220,000+ |
| Senior/Training Captain | Year 15–20+ | £167,000–£205,000 | £220,000–£275,000 |

Notes:
- Sector pay, overnight allowances, and per-diem supplements add £10,000–£25,000+ annually to base figures.
- Time to command at BA mainline is typically 10–15 years due to the size of the seniority list.

Sources: [Aviation A2Z BA 2026](https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2026/04/25/british-airways-pilot-salary-2026/), [AirMappr BA Guide](https://airmappr.com/articles/career/british-airways-pilot-salary-guide), [BA Media Centre](https://mediacentre.britishairways.com/pressrelease/details/17264)

## 4. BALPA Data and Union Context

BALPA (British Airline Pilots Association) is the recognised trade union at British Airways, easyJet, Jet2, and several other UK carriers. Key points:

- BALPA negotiates and publishes pay scales via collective bargaining agreements. The easyJet SO/FO/Captain scale above (Section 2.2) is among the most transparent in UK aviation precisely because it is BALPA-agreed.
- In September 2024, easyJet pilots (via BALPA) were offered a pay rise targeting ~£75,446 at FO Year 5 level. The offer was initially rejected before a revised deal was reached.
- BALPA-represented pilots generally earn more and have stronger employment protections than those on agency or self-employed contracts (a structure more common in some continental European LCC models).
- BALPA's published guidance states short-haul FO salaries range from £35,000 to £60,000 and long-haul FO salaries from £55,000+ at UK carriers — consistent with the data above.
- Flying instructors and early-career turboprop pilots (pre-airline) typically earn £17,000–£35,000.

BALPA salary overview: [https://www.balpa.org/becoming-a-pilot/salary-and-benefits/](https://www.balpa.org/becoming-a-pilot/salary-and-benefits/)

## 5. UK vs Europe vs USA: International Comparison

### 5.1 UK vs Rest of Europe

The UK is a high-paying European aviation market, typically second only to Switzerland and Germany.

| Country | Average Gross Pilot Salary (all roles) |
|---|---|
| Switzerland | €113,672 |
| Germany | €106,000–€150,792 |
| **UK (national average)** | **€90,253 (~£78,000); €115,562 London area** |
| France | €87,903–€111,600 |
| Italy | €80,427 |
| Spain | €77,269 |
| Greece | €56,523 |
| Poland / Czech Republic | ~€50,000 |
| Romania | €32,299 |

Key observations:
- Eastern European LCC bases (e.g. Wizz Air Hungary, Ryanair Poland) pay materially less than UK-contracted equivalents for the same role.
- Western Europe (UK, Germany, France, Netherlands) clusters at the top tier.
- Switzerland is the outlier premium market, driven by Swiss, Edelweiss, and private aviation demand.

Source: [Simple Flying — Salaries of Pilots in Europe 2025](https://simpleflying.com/salaries-pilots-europe-2025/)

### 5.2 UK vs USA

US airline pilots earn significantly more at every career stage. The gap is most pronounced at Captain level.

| Position | UK Typical | USA Typical |
|---|---|---|
| Entry FO (regional / LCC equivalent) | £45,000–£60,000 | $55,000–$85,000 (regional) |
| Major airline FO (mid-career) | £73,000–£115,000 | $120,000–$180,000 |
| Major airline FO Year 1 (Delta/United/AA) | — | ~$113,000–$116,000 |
| Short-haul Captain | £130,000–£172,000 | $200,000–$250,000 |
| Long-haul / widebody Captain | £167,000–£224,000 | $300,000–$420,000+ |

Key contextual factors:
- US pilots are paid per flight hour (not fixed annual salary). A 73-hour month at United generates ~$115,621/year for a Year 1 FO.
- The US has no equivalent to Europe's 900-hour annual FTL cap; higher potential hours mean higher potential earnings.
- UK/European pilots benefit from NHS healthcare, stronger employment law, statutory holidays, and more robust collective agreements — partially offsetting the nominal pay gap.
- After tax, cost of living, and pension adjustments, the gap narrows significantly at FO level but remains large (2–3x) at senior Captain level.
- US median annual wage for airline pilots now exceeds $219,000; major airline captains regularly exceed $300,000.

Sources: [Simple Flying — US vs Europe Comparison](https://simpleflying.com/pilot-salaries-us-europe-compare/), [Simple Flying — US First Officers 2025](https://simpleflying.com/salaries-first-officer-pilots-us-2025/), [Aviation A2Z — American Airlines](https://aviationa2z.com/index.php/2025/09/12/american-airlines-pilot-salary-2024/)

## 6. Typical Career Timeline (UK Short-Haul Composite)

The following represents a composite trajectory for a cadet joining a UK LCC (e.g. easyJet) via a self-funded MPL route.

| Phase | Duration | Gross Pay Range | Key Events |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ab-initio ground school | 6–9 months | £0 | Self-funded or airline-funded; no income |
| Flight training (CPL/MPL) | 9–12 months | £0 | Hours building, type rating |
| Second Officer (SO) | ~2 years | £57,000–£60,000 | No sector pay; loan repayments begin |
| First Officer Year 1–2 | Years 3–4 total | £67,000–£71,000 + ~£15,000 sector | Full FO privileges begin |
| First Officer Year 3–5 | Years 5–7 total | £71,000–£75,000 + sector pay | Building command hours |
| Senior First Officer | Years 7–9 total | £83,000 + sector pay | Command assessment eligibility |
| Captain (upgrade) | Year 8–10 total | £144,000–£152,000 | Significant salary step-change |
| Senior Captain | Year 12+ | £166,000+ with bonuses | Loyalty bonuses; potential TRI/TRE roles |

Notes on timeline variation:
- At legacy carriers like BA, the FO phase is typically longer (10–15 years to command) due to larger seniority lists.
- At high-growth LCCs and regional carriers, command can come within 3–5 years from first qualification.
- A BA Speedbird cadet spends additional years at the reduced-rate SO salary before reaching standard FO pay scales.
